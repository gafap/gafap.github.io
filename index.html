I am an Associate Professor of Economics at Royal Holloway, University of London. I work in applied microeconomics, mainly in health, labour and education economics.

Much of my research studies how the organisation of health care shapes the care patients actually receive — how staffing levels in maternity wards affect delivery decisions, how surgeons’ skills depend on how often they operate, and how financial incentives and information provision change quality in primary care. I also work on the long-term effects of early childhood education, on measuring domestic violence, and on how shared national institutions such as military service shape identity and mobility.

I currently lead EDUCARE, a project on the long-term impacts of early childhood education (ages 0–2) funded by Fundació “la Caixa”, and I coordinate a Horizon Europe project on health care financing systems in Europe.

Before joining Royal Holloway, I taught at the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona. I hold a PhD in Economics from the European University Institute.
